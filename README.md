# swift-calendar-gregorian

Use this package when you need civil dates in the proleptic Gregorian calendar: validating year-month-day values, counting and adding days, months and years, finding weekdays, and converting between a local `Gregorian.DateTime` and a `Time.Instant` in a `Time.Zone` or `Time.Zone.Rule`. For named zones such as Europe/Amsterdam, swift-iana-tz supplies the rule. Start from `Gregorian.Date` for dates, `Gregorian.DateTime` for date and clock time, and `Gregorian.calendar()` for the `Calendar<Gregorian.Date>` value that maps dates to `DayNumber`. For calendar-independent day arithmetic use the `Calendar` package (swift-calendar), and for instants, durations and zones without a calendar use the `Time` package (swift-time).

## Laws

- The calendar is proleptic with astronomical year numbering: year 0 exists and is a leap year, negative years follow the same 4/100/400 rule, and every `Int` is a valid `Gregorian.Year`.
- `DayNumber` 1 is 0001-01-01, so 0000-12-31 is day 0 and 1970-01-01 is day 719 163. `Gregorian.date(on:)` and `Gregorian.dayNumber(of:)` are inverses; every `Int64` day number decodes to a date, and encoding fails only for a date whose day number does not fit `Int64` (years near `Int.min` or `Int.max`). Weekdays are derived from the day number, so they hold for negative years.
- A `Gregorian.Month.Day` is validated against one month and year, and that validity does not transfer: building a `Gregorian.Date` or `Gregorian.DateTime` revalidates the day against the month and year it is used with.
- `Gregorian.Date.adding(months:)` and `adding(years:)` move by calendar months (a year is 12 months) and clamp the day to the target month's length, so 31 January plus one month is the last day of February and a leap day plus one year is 28 February. They fail with overflow when the target year leaves `Int`.
- `Gregorian.Date.next(_:)` is strictly later: it lands 1 to 7 days ahead, a full week when the date is already on that weekday.
- There are no leap seconds: `Gregorian.DateTime` rejects second 60 with `.secondOutOfRange(60)`, because its clock time is a `Time.Minute.Second` (0...59) and the instant timeline has uniform 86 400-second days.
- Zone conversion is exact and lossless: a local date-time is the instant shifted by the zone's offset, nanoseconds are preserved, and `instant(in:)` and `Time.Instant.gregorian(in:)` round-trip for every instant and offset whose result stays inside the `Int64` Unix-second range, including offsets larger than `Int64` seconds. A result outside that range fails with overflow, in either direction. The default zone is UTC.
- A `Time.Zone.Rule` gives the offset in force at each instant. `Time.Instant.gregorian(in:)` and `Gregorian.Date.today(in:at:)` read the local date-time and date with the offset at that instant. `Gregorian.DateTime.instant(in:)` resolves a local time the rule repeats to the earlier instant, and a local time the rule skips to the later one, so 02:30 on a day that jumps from 02:00 to 03:00 is the instant that reads 03:30.
- `Gregorian.Date.start(in:)` is the instant of local midnight under the rule. An ambiguous midnight resolves to the earlier instant; a skipped midnight resolves to the later one, so the day starts at the transition. `Gregorian.Date.instants(in:)` is the half-open range from this day's start to the next day's start, so a day lasts 23 or 25 hours across a transition, and a day the rule skips entirely is an empty range.
- `Gregorian.easter(year:)` is the Gregorian computus for Western Easter Sunday and is defined only from 1583, the first full year of the Gregorian reform.
- `Gregorian.Recurrence` only describes a schedule; nothing in this package expands it into dates.
- When its trait enables `Gregorian.Julian`, `Gregorian.Julian.Day` is the astronomical Julian Day count (days begin at noon UTC), not the Julian calendar. It is stored as `Double`, so conversions keep sub-millisecond, not nanosecond, precision; decoding never produces a negative nanosecond fraction, and converting a `Gregorian.DateTime` revalidates it.
